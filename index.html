How I think about digital change

From startup challenges to digital solutions

Co-founding my own patisserie startup taught me an early lesson: technology is only meaningful when it solves real problems. When the COVID-19 pandemic hit Taiwan shortly after our launch, we had to adapt quickly, shifting from a physical storefront to an online delivery model. To manage the sudden increase in digital inquiries, I built and deployed an automated customer service chatbot on LINE.

Through that experience, I witnessed what business owners truly value in technology: instead of flashy tech gimmicks, they need reliable, intuitive tools that ease daily operational pressures. That is the mindset I bring to digital transformation: pragmatic, customer-centric, and focused on creating tangible value.

I carried the same approach into my internship at Crescendo Lab, where I helped build the digital infrastructure for a B2B tech product launch. By integrating Google Analytics to track user behavior and refining our omnichannel strategy, we engaged more than 400 brands and exceeded our launch target by 25%. Whether I am solving a bottleneck for a single small business or evaluating digital performance for hundreds of B2B clients, I am driven by the same objective: to create digital solutions with measurable, practical impact.

Risk management: innovation within banking

In banking, digital transformation has to respect compliance. You cannot simply move fast and break things. Through my major in Risk Management and my daily work stress-testing portfolios, I have learned to evaluate innovation through a safety-first perspective.

I know how to harness new technologies responsibly. For example, when I trained roughly 100 colleagues on the applications and best practices of Large Language Models, the focus was on maximizing efficiency while strictly maintaining data security. I know how to push digital boundaries without compromising the bank’s core safety rails.

What living across cultures taught me

Rooted in Germany, connected to the world

The German Mittelstand is shaped by Hidden Champions: companies that are rooted locally while competing globally. Supporting them requires a bank that is firmly anchored in Germany while fully fluent in international markets. My own journey reflects that balance. Having studied and worked across Taiwan, France, and Germany, I learned how to connect different perspectives, and ways of working. I am excited to contribute to Commerzbank’s global network, whether by helping streamline cross-border processes, supporting international financial flows, or drawing on my interest in blockchain and digital assets to think about how global banking can become more efficient, secure, and future-oriented.

The international angle: why the IDDP?

What especially attracts me to IDDP is the chance to understand digital transformation in banking from different perspectives before choosing a long-term path. Rotating across units such as product, strategy, technology, and sales would allow me to see how customer needs, business priorities, and digital execution come together across the bank. I am particularly drawn to product-oriented work, because in banking it requires balancing innovation with trust, regulation, and practical value. The opportunity to contribute to places like Neugelb or Comdirect teams makes the program even more exciting, because it offers real exposure to how modern banking evolves through collaboration, experimentation, and execution.
